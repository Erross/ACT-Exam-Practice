About this project

What this practice site does — and does not do.

This is a free, unofficial practice resource for the enhanced ACT. Questions, passages, and synthetic data sets are original practice material, not copied ACT test questions.

What is available

Timed practice is available for English, Mathematics, Reading, and optional Science. You can practice one section or run a full attempt in English → Mathematics → Reading order, with Science afterward when selected. The multiple-choice bank contains original practice material designed against the current enhanced ACT structure.

Each section has a preflight screen before its timer begins, question navigation, flags for review, saved-attempt recovery, a warning before submitting unanswered or flagged questions, and post-submit explanations. Full-test section scores remain hidden until the complete attempt ends.

Current enhanced ACT model

The application models English (50 questions/35 minutes), Mathematics (45/50), Reading (36/40), and optional Science (40/40). Displayed item counts include embedded field-test items that are not included in the scored raw result. Mathematics is the only section in this app where a calculator is permitted. The real online ACT provides Desmos; this practice site does not embed a calculator.

Estimated scores

Section estimates use raw-to-scale conversion tables from two official enhanced ACT online practice tests. Because real ACT forms are equated independently, and because this site’s questions are unofficial, every result is explicitly labeled an estimate and shows the practice-form range where the official conversion tables differ.

The estimated Composite uses English, Mathematics, and Reading. Science does not affect the Composite. When Science is taken, the app also reports an estimated STEM score based on Mathematics and Science.

What is not supported

ACT Writing is not currently supported. This first release is focused on the multiple-choice sections. The site does not provide an official ACT score, reproduce secure ACT questions, or claim that its synthetic practice forms have been psychometrically equated like operational ACT forms.
